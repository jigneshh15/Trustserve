# TrustServe — Startup and E-Commerce Brief

## Problem
Customers can find it difficult to identify dependable local service professionals, understand fair pricing and coordinate home-service visits. Local professionals may also lack predictable bookings and transparent platform terms.

## Target users
1. Residents and families in housing societies.
2. Local cleaning, plumbing and electrical professionals.
3. Students and families seeking tutoring.

## Value proposition
A trust-first local service marketplace combining skill verification, transparent price bands, society-level group booking, SafeVisit visit updates and clear professional earning rules.

## Revenue model
- Commission on completed bookings (illustrative assumption: 10–15%; validate with users).
- Optional professional subscription for enhanced visibility.
- Society group service partnerships.
No real payment is enabled in this prototype.

## Lean Canvas
- Problem: trust, uncertain prices, fragmented discovery.
- Customer segments: households, societies, local professionals.
- Unique value: verified local professionals + group booking + safety workflow.
- Solution: searchable service catalogue, booking, price band, SafeVisit.
- Channels: housing societies, referrals, local digital campaigns.
- Revenue: booking commission, optional pro subscription.
- Costs: cloud hosting, support, verification operations, marketing.
- Key metrics: completed bookings, repeat rate, cancellation rate, pro retention, dispute rate.
- Unfair advantage: society-level booking density and local trust operations (hypothesis to validate).

## SWOT
Strengths: focused local trust positioning; society group bookings.
Weaknesses: operationally intensive verification; early marketplace liquidity challenge.
Opportunities: underserved smaller-city local services; repeat household needs.
Threats: established aggregators, quality incidents, price competition, regulatory obligations.

## PESTEL
Political: startup and consumer-protection policy changes.
Economic: household spending and professional income volatility.
Social: trust, safety and word-of-mouth influence.
Technological: mobile access, cloud, secure payments and identity tooling.
Environmental: route consolidation through group visits may reduce repeat travel.
Legal: privacy, consent, consumer protection, worker terms, tax and payment compliance.

## MVP
Launch in one locality with two categories first, verify a small professional cohort, run society pilot, measure repeat bookings and safety outcomes before expansion.

## IP and compliance
Use original code and licensed assets. Publish clear privacy notice, consent and cancellation terms. Avoid fake reviews and manipulative dark patterns. Obtain qualified legal advice before launch.
